## Section 1
## Foundations of English Grammar and Vocabulary

### 1.1 Sentence Architecture

A **sentence** is the **largest grammatical unit** composed of words, phrases, and clauses. A valid sentence must:
- Make **complete sense**
- Contain at least **one subject** and one **finite verb**
- Serve a **definite communicative function**

---

#### Core Elements of a Sentence

| Element | Role |
|---|---|
| **Subject** | Who or what the sentence is about |
| **Verb** | The action or state |
| **Object** | Receives the action of the verb |
| **Adjunct** | Added information; **not essential** to basic structure (e.g., time, place, manner) |
| **Complement** | Completes the meaning of the subject or object |

> 📌 **Subject Complement:** A word or phrase that **follows a linking verb** and describes the subject.
> _Example:_ "She **is brilliant.**" — "brilliant" is the subject complement.

> 📌 **Adjunct:** Extra, removable information added to a sentence.
> _Example:_ "She studies **every night.**" — "every night" is an adjunct.

### 1.2 Classification of Clauses

| Clause Type | Can Stand Alone? | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Independent Clause** | ✅ Yes | Forms a complete sentence on its own | *"I am a student."* |
| **Subordinate / Dependent Clause** | ❌ No | Depends on a main clause; functions as adjective, adverb, or noun | *"…because I was late"* |
| **Relative Clause** | ❌ No | Begins with **who / which / that**; identifies or describes a noun | *"The book **that I borrowed**…"* |

**Three types of Subordinate Clauses:**
- **Adjective Clause** — modifies a noun (e.g., *"the man who called"*)
- **Adverbial Clause** — modifies a verb or sentence (e.g., *"although it rained"*)
- **Noun Clause** — acts as a noun (e.g., *"what he said"*)

### 1.3 Word Formation

| Process | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Acronym** | Initial letters of a phrase form a new word | **RAM** = Random Access Memory |
| **Blending** | Parts of two words are joined | **smog** = smoke + fog |

### 1.4 Phonetics

- **Phonetics** is the study of the sounds of human speech.
- Includes:
  - **Word Stress** — which syllable in a word receives emphasis
  - **IPA (International Phonetic Alphabet)** — a standard system for transcribing pronunciation
  - **Monophthongs** — pure, single vowel sounds (no glide); represented in phonetic diagrams

### 1.5 The Article System

| Article | Type | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the** | Definite | Specific or unique reference | *"the sun," "the President"* |
| **a / an** | Indefinite | Non-specific, first mention | *"a book," "an idea"* |
| *(none)* | Zero Article | Generalizations, proper nouns | *"Water is essential," "Dhaka is a city"* |

> 💡 Use **"a"** before consonant sounds and **"an"** before vowel sounds — based on **sound**, not spelling.
> _e.g.,_ "an hour" (h is silent), "a university" (sounds like "yoo-niversity")


### 1.6 Modal Auxiliaries

**Modals** are helping verbs used to express **time, mood**, or **degrees of certainty**.

| Modal | Common Use |
|---|---|
| **can / could** | Ability, possibility |
| **may / might** | Permission, weaker possibility |
| **should** | Advice, expectation |
| **must / have to** | Strong obligation |
| **would** | Conditional, politeness |

> 📌 **Hedging in Academic Writing:**
> Modals are essential for **hedging** — softening claims to show caution and intellectual honesty.
> _Examples:_ "This **may** suggest…", "The results **could** indicate…", "It **might** be argued that…"

## Section 2
## Reading Strategies and Information Processing

Effective reading requires selecting the **right technique** based on your purpose.

| Strategy | Purpose | How It Works |
|---|---|---|
| **Skimming** | Get the **overall gist** quickly | Read headings, first/last sentences of paragraphs, bold text |
| **Scanning** | Find **specific information** | Search for keywords, dates, names — don't read every word |
| **Intensive Reading** | **Master a topic** or develop research skills | Rigorous, slow, detailed reading with full comprehension |
| **Extensive Reading** | Improve **general fluency** and vocabulary | Read widely for pleasure; don't stop at every unknown word |

> 💡 **Exam Tip:** Skimming + Scanning are ideal for **MCQ / comprehension passages**. Intensive reading is for **note-making and critical analysis**.

## Section 3
## Advanced Academic Writing

### 3.1 Key Features of Academic Style

| Feature | Description | What to Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Formality** | Professional, impersonal tone | Colloquialisms, contractions (*"can't," "don't"*), slang |
| **Complexity** | Noun-based phrases, subordinate clauses for logical connection | Simple, choppy sentences |
| **Hedging** | Indirect language to distinguish fact from interpretation | Overconfident absolute claims |

**Hedging Expressions:**
- *"It is believed that…"*
- *"The data possibly suggests…"*
- *"This may be attributed to…"*
- *"It appears that…"*


### 3.2 The Writing Process

Academic writing is **not a single act** — it is a structured process:

```
Planning → Generating Ideas → Organizing → Drafting → Revising (based on feedback)
```

| Stage | What Happens |
|---|---|
| **Planning** | Define purpose, audience, scope |
| **Generating Ideas** | Brainstorming, mind-mapping, freewriting |
| **Organizing** | Outline structure; arrange arguments logically |
| **Drafting** | Write the first complete version |
| **Revising** | Improve based on feedback; check clarity, coherence, correctness |


### 3.3 Synthesis Writing

**Synthesis** = integrating information from **multiple sources** to support a single thesis.

| Type | Goal | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Explanatory Synthesis** | Elaborate on a topic | Combine facts and explanations from multiple sources objectively |
| **Argumentative Synthesis** | Persuade the reader | Take a **definite position** on a debatable issue; use evidence to argue |


### 3.4 Academic Integrity and Referencing

- **Plagiarism** — copying directly from sources **without acknowledgment**. This is a serious academic offence.
- **Why Reference?**
  - To give **credit** to original authors
  - To allow readers to **verify** data and sources
  - To demonstrate the **breadth** of your research

**Common Referencing Styles:**

| Style | Typically Used In |
|---|---|
| **APA** (American Psychological Association) | Social sciences, CSE, Engineering |
| **MLA** (Modern Language Association) | Humanities, Literature |

> ✅ Both require **in-text citations** AND a **bibliography / reference list** at the end.

## Section 4
## Paragraph Development

### 4.1 Structure of a Good Paragraph

A well-developed paragraph follows this pattern: (Topic Sentence + Supporting Details)

```
Background Information → Core Event / Main Point → Conclusion / Wrap-up
```

The **Topic Sentence** is the **most important sentence** in the paragraph:
- States the **purpose and viewpoint** of the paragraph
- Usually placed **at the beginning**
- All other sentences should **support or develop** it


### 4.2 Narrative Paragraphs

**Purpose:** Tell a story, usually arranged **chronologically**.

A narrative paragraph must contain:

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| **Central Idea** | The main point or message of the story |
| **Characters** | The people involved |
| **Plot** | The sequence of events; includes **conflict** and **resolution** |
| **Setting** | Time and place where the story occurs |


### 4.3 Argumentative Paragraphs

**Purpose:** Present and defend a position using evidence.

**Core Structure:**

| Part | Role |
|---|---|
| **Claim** | The main point / assertion being made |
| **Evidence** | Scientific data, statistics, expert quotes supporting the claim |
| **Counter-argument** | Acknowledging the opposing view |
| **Rebuttal / Refutation** | Responding to the counter-argument |

**Two Ways to Handle Counter-Arguments:**

| Technique | What It Does |
|---|---|
| **Rebuttal** | Shows the opposing argument is **wrong** |
| **Refutation** | **Partially agrees** with the opponent but demonstrates your point is **more convincing** |

## Section 5
## Technical and Professional Communication

### 5.1 Technical Reports

#### Types of Technical Reports

| Report Type | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Feasibility Report** | Investigates and evaluates the **viability** of a proposed project |
| **Primary Research Report** | Details the process and findings of an **experiment** or original research |
| **Technical Background Report** | Provides comprehensive **specifics on a topic** (e.g., mobile phone technology) |

#### Standard Report Structure

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **Title Page** | Report title, author, date, institution |
| **Abstract** | Concise summary of the entire report |
| **Content List** | Table of contents with page numbers |
| **Introduction** | Background, objectives, scope |
| **Main Body** | Methodology, Findings, Analysis / Discussion |
| **Conclusion** | Summary of findings and recommendations |
| **References** | All cited sources in proper format |


### 5.2 Business Correspondence

#### Business Letters

Formal professional documents used for **external communication**.

| Style | Format |
|---|---|
| **Full Block** | All text (including date and closing) **left-justified** |
| **Modified Block** | Date, complimentary close, and signature **shifted to the right** |

**Standard Parts of a Business Letter:**
Date → Inside Address → Salutation → Body Paragraphs → Complimentary Close → Signature


#### Memorandums (Memos)

- **Purpose:** Short official notes for **internal communication** within an organisation.
- **Format:** Begins with: `TO:` / `FROM:` / `DATE:` / `SUBJECT:`
- **Example:** *A memo from the Head of IT to all staff announcing a Software Training program on problem-solving with software tools.*


### 5.3 Administrative Documents

#### Meeting Management

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Agenda** | Lists the **sequence of discussion items** to be covered in a meeting; distributed before the meeting |
| **Meeting Minutes** | The **instant written record** of decisions made and action items assigned during the meeting |

#### Press Releases

- **Purpose:** Announce news formally (e.g., a product launch, an event).
- **Length:** **300–800 words**
- **Style:** Professional, succinct
- **Voice:** Must use **active voice** (e.g., *"The company launched…"* not *"The product was launched by…"*)

## Section 6
## Career Development

### 6.1 Curriculum Vitae (CV)

A **CV** is a structured, formal document summarising your professional and academic profile.

**Standard Sections:**

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **Career Objective** | A brief statement of your professional goals |
| **Academic Background** | Degrees, institutions, years (e.g., *B.Sc. in CSE, University of Rajshahi*) |
| **Training / Certifications** | Relevant courses and technical skills |
| **Language Proficiency** | Languages spoken and level of proficiency |


### 6.2 Cover Letter

- A **one-page document** sent alongside a CV/resume.
- Explains **why you are specifically qualified** for the job.
- More personal and job-specific than a CV.

**Active vs. Passive Voice — Always Prefer Active:**

| ✅ Active Voice (Preferred) | ❌ Passive Voice (Avoid) |
|---|---|
| *"I developed the software."* | *"The software was developed by me."* |
| *"I managed a team of five."* | *"A team of five was managed by me."* |

> 💡 Active voice makes your writing **direct, confident, and engaging** — exactly the impression you want to make on an employer.

---
### 6.3 Quotations

A **quotation** is a document provided by a seller to a buyer offering goods or services at a specific price and under stated conditions.

- **Purpose:** It allows a business to compare several sellers to find the most cost-effective and highest-quality source of supply.
- **Requirements:** When inviting a quotation, you must clearly specify the **quantity, technical details, delivery timeline**, and the period for which the quoted price must remain valid.

### 6.4 Tender Notice

A **tender notice** is a formal request inviting potential suppliers to submit an application to deliver specific goods or perform a large-scale job (like building a bridge) for an agreed price.

- **Key Characteristics:** Unlike quotations, which are sought from selected sellers, tender notices are often **advertised publicly** in newspapers.
- **Components:** It must include the **exact nature of the job, estimated cost, submission deadline**, and the amount of **earnest money** (bid security) required to be deposited.
