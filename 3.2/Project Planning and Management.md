## Chapter 1
## Introduction to Project Management

### 1.1 Fundamentals

**Definition of a Project:** A project is a temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result.

**Key Characteristics:**
- **Unique Outcome:** Every project produces a result that has not existed before in its specific context.
- **Temporary Nature:** Projects have a defined beginning and end date, completing when goals are met or the project is no longer viable.

**Operations vs. Projects:** Operations are ongoing and repetitive (e.g., monthly payroll), while projects are one-time efforts to create or modify systems (e.g., developing a new payroll software).

**Project Constraints (Triple Constraint):** Projects are limited by Scope, Schedule, and Cost, often referred to as the Iron Triangle. Secondary constraints include Quality, Resources, and Risk.

**Project Success:** "Project success" focuses on the impact of the final product on stakeholders, while "Project management success" focuses on meeting scope, budget, time, and quality parameters.

---

### 1.2 Role of Project Managers and Quality Management

**Role of project managers:** Project managers are responsible for planning, executing, monitoring, controlling, and closing projects. They serve as the primary point of contact for stakeholders, lead the project team, manage resources, balance constraints, and ensure that project objectives are met.

**Qualities they need:** Project managers need leadership skills, communication skills, problem-solving abilities, emotional intelligence, adaptability, negotiation skills, technical knowledge, risk management ability, decision-making capability, and the ability to motivate teams.

**The central role of quality management in project planning and control:** Quality management ensures that project deliverables meet the required standards and satisfy stakeholder expectations. It involves quality planning (defining standards), quality assurance (auditing processes), and quality control (inspecting deliverables). Quality management is central to project planning and control because poor quality leads to rework, delays, cost overruns, and customer dissatisfaction.

## Chapter 2
## Strategy, Objectives, and Project Selection

### 2.1 Strategic Alignment

**Strategic Alignment:** Projects are vehicles for delivering organizational strategy. Organizations use Vision (aspirational future), Mission (current purpose), and Goals to guide selection.

**SMART Objectives:** To be effective, objectives must be Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound.

**The Business Case:** A document that justifies the project based on problems or opportunities, providing the basis for authorization.

---

### 2.2 Selection Models

**Selection Models:**
- **Economic:** Net Present Value (NPV), Internal Rate of Return (IRR), and Payback Period.
- **Scoring:** Weighted scoring models used to compare projects based on multiple criteria.

## Chapter 3
## Project Initiation

### 3.1 Project Charter and Environment

**Project Charter:** A formal document issued by a sponsor that authorizes the project's existence and gives the project manager authority to apply resources.

**Project Environment:**
- **Internal:** Organizational culture, structure, and process assets.
- **External:** Market conditions, legal regulations, and social-economic factors.

**Project Management Office (PMO):** A structure that standardizes governance. PMOs can be Supportive (consultative), Controlling (require compliance), or Directive (directly manage projects).

---

### 3.2 Project Life-Cycle

**Project Life-Cycle:** The project life cycle covers the entire span of a project from its start to its end. It includes the processes concerned with project justification and initiation, planning, execution, monitoring and controlling, and closing.

**Identify project start and end:** The project start is formally marked by the issuance of the project charter; the project end is marked by formal acceptance of deliverables and closing of project accounts.

**Processes concerned with project justification and initiation:** These include developing the business case, conducting feasibility studies, identifying stakeholders, defining high-level scope, and obtaining authorization to begin the project.

**Typical steps in conventional project and system life cycles:**
- **Conventional project life cycle:** Initiation → Planning → Execution → Monitoring and Controlling → Closing.
- **System life cycle (for software/system development):** Feasibility → Requirements → Design → Development → Testing → Deployment → Maintenance.

## Chapter 4
## Planning and Scope Management

### 4.1 Requirements Management

**Requirements Elicitation:** The process of discovering needs through discussion, research, and observation. Requirements can be Functional (what the system does) or Non-functional (system qualities like security).

**Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM):** A grid linking requirements to their origin and the deliverables that satisfy them.

---

### 4.2 Work Breakdown Structure and Scope Creep

**Work Breakdown Structure (WBS):** A hierarchical decomposition of the total scope of work into smaller, manageable "work packages".

**Scope Creep:** The uncontrolled expansion of project scope without adjustments to time, cost, or resources.

---

## Chapter 5
## Stakeholder and Communication Management

### 5.1 Stakeholder Analysis

**Stakeholder Analysis:** Categorizing stakeholders by Power and Interest to create engagement strategies.

**Communication Types:**
- **Synchronous:** Real-time (e.g., meetings, phone calls).
- **Asynchronous:** Delayed (e.g., emails, blogs, reports).

**RACI Chart:** A responsibility assignment matrix defining who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed for tasks.

## Chapter 6
## Leadership and Team Management

### 6.1 Leadership and Team Development

**Leadership Styles:** Includes Servant Leadership (focusing on team needs), Transactional, and Transformational approaches.

**Tuckman's Ladder of Team Development:** The stages of team growth: Forming, Storming, Norming, Performing, and Adjourning.

**Emotional Intelligence (EQ):** The ability to identify and manage personal and others' emotions to improve team performance.

## Chapter 7
## Project Scheduling and Estimation

### 7.1 Activity Sequencing

**Activity Sequencing:** Identifying logical relationships such as Finish-to-Start (FS), Start-to-Start (SS), etc.

### 7.2 Estimation

**Estimation in project management:** Estimation is the process of predicting the effort, duration, cost, and resources required to complete project activities. It combines quantitative tools with qualitative judgment to produce reliable forecasts.

**Estimation Methodology:** The methodology for estimation includes: defining the scope, selecting appropriate estimation techniques (analogous, parametric, three-point, bottom-up), collecting historical data, applying models, validating estimates with experts, and refining estimates as more information becomes available.

**Formal models for size Estimation:** Formal size estimation models include:
- **Lines of Code (LOC) estimation:** Based on expected source lines of code.
- **Function Point Analysis (FPA):** Measures software size based on functional requirements (inputs, outputs, inquiries, files, interfaces).
- **Use Case Points (UCP):** Estimates size based on actors and use cases.
- **COCOMO (Constructive Cost Model):** A parametric model that uses size (KLOC) to predict effort and schedule.

**Translating size into effort:** Size is converted to effort using productivity rates (e.g., person-hours per function point) or algorithmic models (e.g., COCOMO: Effort = a × (Size)^b). Historical data from past projects is used to calibrate the translation.

**Translating effort into schedule:** Effort is converted to schedule using models such as:
- **COCOMO schedule equation:** Schedule = c × (Effort)^d
- **Putnam's model:** Based on the software life cycle and manpower distribution.
- **Rule of thumb:** For small projects, schedule (months) ≈ 2.5 × (person-months)^(1/3)

---

### 7.3 Critical Path Method

**Critical Path Method (CPM):** Identifying the sequence of activities that determines the shortest possible project duration; any delay on this path delays the project.

**Float (Slack):** The amount of time an activity can be delayed without delaying the project.

> 📌 **Comparison:** Analogous estimation uses similar past projects; parametric estimation uses statistical relationships (e.g., cost per square foot); three-point estimation calculates an average of Optimistic, Pessimistic, and Most Likely estimates; bottom-up estimation aggregates estimates from WBS work packages.

## Chapter 8
## Resource Management and Schedule Compression

### 8.1 Resource Management

**Resource Allocation:** Assigning human and physical resources to activities.

**Resource Leveling:** Adjusting dates to address resource overallocation, often extending the schedule.

**Schedule Compression:** Shortening duration without reducing scope via Crashing (adding resources) or Fast Tracking (performing activities in parallel).

## Chapter 9
## Budget and Procurement

### 9.1 Budget Management

**Cost Types:** Direct costs (labor/materials for the project) vs. Indirect costs (overhead).

**Procurement Cycle:** Identifying needs, selecting vendors through Request for Quote (RFQ) or Request for Proposal (RFP), and managing contracts.

**Contract Types:** Fixed-Price (set price), Cost-Reimbursable (pays actual costs plus fee), and Time and Materials (T&M).

## Chapter 10
## Risk Management

### 10.1 Risk Management Cycle and Identification

**Risk Management:** Risk management is the systematic process of identifying, analyzing, responding to, and monitoring risks that could affect project objectives. It includes the management cycle, identification tools, quantifications, monitoring, and mitigation, especially in global project teams.

**Management cycle:** The risk management cycle consists of: Risk Identification → Risk Assessment (Qualitative and Quantitative) → Risk Response Planning → Risk Monitoring and Control → (repeat) Risk Identification for new risks.

**Identification tools:** Risk identification tools include: brainstorming, Delphi technique, interviews, root cause analysis, SWOT analysis (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats), checklists, assumption analysis, and diagramming techniques (e.g., cause-and-effect diagrams, flowcharts).

**Quantifications:** Risk quantification evaluates the probability and impact of identified risks. Techniques include:
- **Probability and Impact Matrix:** Ranks risks by multiplying probability × impact.
- **Expected Monetary Value (EMV):** EMV = Probability × Impact (in monetary terms).
- **Decision Tree Analysis:** Models decisions and their possible outcomes.
- **Monte Carlo Simulation:** Runs thousands of simulations to predict outcomes.

**Monitoring:** Risk monitoring involves tracking identified risks, identifying new risks, executing risk response plans, and evaluating the effectiveness of risk responses throughout the project life cycle.

**Mitigation in global project teams:** Risk mitigation in global teams addresses challenges such as time zone differences, cultural differences, language barriers, communication delays, and legal/regulatory variations. Mitigation strategies include: using collaboration tools, overlapping work hours, clear communication protocols, cultural training, distributed agile practices, and regular virtual meetings.

---

### 10.2 Risk Analysis and Response Strategies

**Risk Analysis:** Evaluating risks by Probability and Impact.

**Strategies for Threats:** Avoid, Transfer, Mitigate, or Accept.

**Strategies for Opportunities:** Exploit, Share, Enhance, or Accept.

## Chapter 11
## Monitoring and Controlling

### 11.1 Qualitative and Quantitative Monitoring

**Qualitative Monitoring:** Regularly tracking non-financial knowledge areas like scope and quality.

**Earned Value Management (EVM):** A quantitative tool measuring performance.
- **CV (Cost Variance) = EV - AC**
- **SV (Schedule Variance) = EV - PV**
- **CPI (Cost Performance Index) = EV / AC**

> 💡 **EVM Logic:** If an index (CPI/SPI) is < 1.0, the project is behind schedule or over budget.

**Change Control:** A formal process to approve or reject modifications to baselines.

## Chapter 12
## Agile Software Development

### 12.1 Agile Fundamentals

**Agile Software Development:** Agile software development is an approach that values flexibility, collaboration, and customer feedback over rigid planning and documentation. It focuses on delivering working software in short iterations.

**Cost of Change:** In traditional development, the cost of change increases exponentially over time. In agile development, the cost of change is flattened through iterative development, continuous testing, and frequent feedback, making changes less expensive even late in the project.

**Agile Manifesto:** The Agile Manifesto prioritizes individuals and interactions over processes and tools, working software over comprehensive documentation, customer collaboration over contract negotiation, and responding to change over following a plan.

**Agile principles (from the Agile Manifesto):**
1. Highest priority is customer satisfaction through early and continuous delivery.
2. Welcome changing requirements, even late in development.
3. Deliver working software frequently (weeks rather than months).
4. Business people and developers work together daily.
5. Build projects around motivated individuals; give them support and trust.
6. Face-to-face conversation is the most efficient way to convey information.
7. Working software is the primary measure of progress.
8. Agile processes promote sustainable development; sponsors, developers, and users should maintain a constant pace indefinitely.
9. Continuous attention to technical excellence and good design enhances agility.
10. Simplicity — maximizing the amount of work not done — is essential.
11. The best architectures, requirements, and designs emerge from self-organizing teams.
12. At regular intervals, the team reflects on how to become more effective, then tunes and adjusts its behavior accordingly.

**Benefits of agile development:** Benefits include: faster time to market, higher customer satisfaction, better product quality, increased flexibility to change, improved team morale, reduced risk, and greater transparency.

**Challenges of agile development:** Challenges include: difficulty with distributed teams, need for customer availability, less predictability of final cost/date, risk of scope creep, requires experienced team members, may produce less documentation, and organizational resistance to change.

**Agile frameworks, values, and principles:** Agile frameworks include Scrum, XP (Extreme Programming), Lean, Kanban, Crystal, and FDD (Feature-Driven Development). Common agile values align with the Agile Manifesto; principles guide decision-making in each framework.

**Continuous Integration (CI):** Continuous Integration is the practice of frequently merging all developers' working copies to a shared mainline (often several times per day). Each merge triggers an automated build and test process to detect integration errors immediately.

**Testing strategy in agile:** Agile testing strategy includes: test-driven development (write tests before code), automated unit tests, integration tests, regression tests, acceptance tests, and exploratory testing. Testing is continuous throughout each iteration.

**High Level Scrum Process:** The high-level Scrum process includes: a prioritized Product Backlog, Sprint Planning to select backlog items for the Sprint, a Sprint (timeboxed iteration of 1-4 weeks), Daily Standups for coordination, a potentially shippable Increment at the end of the Sprint, a Sprint Review to demo work, and a Sprint Retrospective to improve the process.
