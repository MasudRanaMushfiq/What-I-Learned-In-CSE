## Chapter 1

## Fundamentals of Cyberspace and Cyber Law

### 1.1 Definitions and Scope

**Cyberspace:** The virtual environment created by interconnected computer systems, networks, and the internet where digital data is exchanged and processed.

**Cyber Law:** Also known as internet law, it is the legal framework that regulates activities in cyberspace, governing digital transactions, data protection, and the prevention of cybercrimes.

**Cybersecurity:** Specifically refers to the security of digital devices, computers, or entire computer systems.

---

### 1.2 Importance and Utility of Cyber Law

Cyber law is essential because conventional laws often struggle to address the unique nature of the virtual world. Its importance includes:

- **Intangible Dimension:** Cyberspace is a dimension impossible to regulate using physical-world laws.
- **Jurisdictional Boundaries:** Cybercriminals can operate across borders instantly; for example, a person in Bangladesh could hack a bank in the USA and transfer funds to Switzerland in minutes.
- **Protection of Mass Users:** It ensures the safety of electronic transactions, protects mass users from violations, and provides redress to victims.

> 📌 **Cyber Law** is sometimes called "internet law" — it is the legal framework that regulates all activities in cyberspace, covering digital transactions, data protection, and cybercrime prevention. Conventional physical-world laws are insufficient to address the unique, borderless nature of the virtual world.

---

### 1.3 Nature, Origin, and Development of Cyber Law and the Internet

The internet began as a research network (ARPANET) and evolved into a global public infrastructure. As digital interactions grew — covering commerce, communication, and governance — the need for dedicated legal frameworks emerged. Cyber law developed to address the new legal challenges posed by:

- The **borderless** and **anonymous** nature of online activity
- The **speed** of digital transactions across multiple jurisdictions
- The rise of new forms of crime and harm unique to the digital environment

|Aspect|Description|
|---|---|
|**Nature**|Cyber law is dynamic, interdisciplinary, and crosses traditional legal boundaries|
|**Scope**|Covers electronic commerce, digital signatures, data protection, cybercrime, privacy, and intellectual property online|
|**Utility**|Provides legal certainty for digital transactions, protects citizens online, and enables prosecution of cybercriminals|

## Chapter 2

## Cybercrime: Types, Causes, and Criminals

### 2.1 Defining Cybercrime and Jurisdiction

Cybercrime is defined as any unlawful act where a computer or communication device is used as a **tool**, a **target**, or both.

**Jurisdiction and Cyber Crime:** Because cybercriminals can operate from any country and target victims anywhere in the world, jurisdiction is one of the most complex issues in cyber law. A crime committed digitally in one country can have consequences in another, making cross-border cooperation and legal frameworks essential.

---

### 2.2 Classification of Cybercrime

1. **Against Individuals:** Phishing, cyberstalking, and identity theft.
2. **Against Organizations:** Hacking company databases and DoS attacks.
3. **Against Property:** Credit card fraud and Intellectual Property (IP) theft.
4. **Against Government/Society:** Cyber terrorism and espionage.

---

### 2.3 Key Types of Cybercrime

**Hacking:** Illegal intrusion into a computer system or network to destroy, alter, or steal data.

**Phishing:** Fraudulent attempts to obtain sensitive information (passwords, PINs) by posing as a trustworthy entity, usually via email.

- _Example:_ An email appearing to be from a bank asking a user to click a "verify account" link that captures their credentials.

**DoS and DDoS Attacks:**

|Attack Type|Description|
|---|---|
|**DoS (Denial of Service)**|Flooding a system with requests from a **single source** to crash it|
|**DDoS (Distributed Denial of Service)**|A larger-scale attack using multiple compromised systems (**botnets**) to flood a target simultaneously|

**Malware:** Malicious software including:

- **Viruses:** Programs that infect clean files and spread uncontrollably.
- **Worms:** Self-replicating programs that spread across whole networks.
- **Trojan Horse:** Malware masquerading as legitimate software to create security backdoors.

**Salami Attacks:** Alterations so insignificant (e.g., deducting 10 cents from thousands of accounts) that they go unnoticed in individual cases but result in massive total theft.

**Data Diddling:** Changing raw data before or during input into a computer system (e.g., altering financial records before processing).

**Logic Bomb:** Code intentionally inserted into software that executes a malicious function only when specific conditions are met.

---

### 2.4 Invasion of Privacy, Defamation, and E-Mail Abuse

**Invasion of Privacy:** The unauthorized collection, disclosure, or misuse of an individual's personal information in cyberspace.

- **Constitutional Basis of Privacy:** The right to privacy is recognized as a fundamental right, and this constitutional basis extends to digital spaces, providing grounds for legal protection against unauthorized surveillance and data collection.
- **Unsolicited e-Mail (Spam):** The sending of bulk or unwanted electronic messages without the recipient's consent, which is regulated under cyber law.
- **Defamation:** Publishing false statements online that damage a person's reputation. Cyber law covers both libel (written defamation) and content posted on digital platforms.
- **Harassment and e-Mail Abuse:** Repeated unwanted contact or threatening communication via electronic mail, punishable under cyber law provisions.
- **Present Legal Protection:** Current laws provide mechanisms for victims of privacy invasion, defamation, and harassment to seek legal redress, including removal of content, compensation, and criminal prosecution.

> 📌 **Cyberstalking** is a specific form of harassment where the perpetrator uses the internet or digital communication tools to stalk, intimidate, or harass an individual. It falls within the broader category of crimes against individuals.

---

### 2.5 Categories of Cybercriminals

|Category|Description|
|---|---|
|**Hackers**|Individuals who break into systems; can be **White Hat** (ethical, for testing) or **Black Hat** (malicious)|
|**Crackers**|Malicious individuals who bypass security specifically for theft or damage|
|**Insider Threats**|Employees or partners who misuse their authorized access to leak data or cause harm|
|**Script Kiddies**|Inexperienced hackers using pre-made tools|

---

### 2.6 Criminal Justice in Bangladesh and Implications on Cybercrime

Bangladesh has enacted specific legislation to address cybercrime and its implications. The criminal justice system addresses cyber offenses through specialized tribunals and agencies. The implications of cybercrime in Bangladesh include financial loss, damage to national security, erosion of public trust in digital systems, and violations of individual rights. Protection of Copyrights and Intellectual Property rights in cyberspace is also a key area of concern within the criminal justice framework.

## Chapter 3

## Bangladesh Cyber Law Framework and ICT Policy

### 3.1 ICT Act 2006

The first major act in Bangladesh to provide legal recognition and security for ICT.

- **Objective:** Facilitate electronic commerce and provide a legal framework for digital signatures.
- **Digital Signature:** Uses **Public Key Infrastructure (PKI)** and cryptography to ensure authenticity and integrity of electronic records.

|Provision|Details|
|---|---|
|**Legal Recognition**|Grants electronic records and digital signatures the same legal standing as physical documents|
|**Digital Signature**|Uses PKI and cryptography to ensure authenticity and integrity|
|**Scope**|Covers electronic commerce, electronic filing, and electronic governance|

---

### 3.2 Digital Security Act (DSA) 2018

This act focuses on digital security, identification, and suppression of offenses.

- **Critical Information Infrastructure (CII):** Government-declared vital systems (e.g., banking, power grids) whose damage would harm national security or public health.
- **Extra-Territorial Application:** The act applies to offenses committed outside Bangladesh if they use digital devices situated within Bangladesh or target the country.

**Legal Effects of Electronic Evidence under DSA 2018:**

- Digital evidence is admissible in court if it meets requirements of authenticity, reliability, completeness, and believability.
- The act provides a legal framework for collecting, preserving, and presenting electronic evidence in cyber tribunals.

---

### 3.3 Specialized Authorities

|Authority|Role|
|---|---|
|**National Cyber Security Agency**|Established for carrying out the purposes of the act and overseeing national cybersecurity|
|**Digital Forensic Lab**|Specialized facilities under the Agency for examining digital evidence and reconstructing cyber incidents|
|**Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT)**|Experts who ensure emergency security for CII and take remedial measures during digital attacks|
|**Cyber Tribunal**|Specialized court for speedy and effective trial of cyber offenses|

---

### 3.4 ICT Policy in Bangladesh

**e-Readiness in Bangladesh** refers to the state of preparedness of Bangladesh to participate in digital and internet-driven activities. The national ICT policy covers the following key areas:

|Area|Description|
|---|---|
|**e-Commerce**|Legal framework and digital infrastructure supporting online buying and selling of goods and services within Bangladesh|
|**e-Governance**|Application of ICT to improve government services — covering Government-to-Citizen (G2C), Government-to-Business (G2B), Government-to-Government (G2G), and Government-to-Employee (G2E) interactions. _Example:_ Online tax payment and NID smart card systems|
|**e-Learning / Education**|Use of digital technologies and internet platforms to deliver education and learning materials to students across Bangladesh|
|**e-Journal**|Digital publication and dissemination of academic and research journals through online platforms|
|**e-Voting**|The use of electronic systems to conduct voting processes, currently at various stages of study and development in Bangladesh|

> 💡 **e-Governance** is the application of ICT to improve government services. It operates across four interaction types: G2C (Government to Citizen), G2B (Government to Business), G2G (Government to Government), and G2E (Government to Employee).

## Chapter 4

## Digital Evidence and E-Systems

### 4.1 Digital Evidence

Information of value to an investigation stored or transmitted by an electronic device.

**Characteristics:** To be admissible, digital evidence must be:

- **Authentic** — it must be what it claims to be
- **Reliable** — the process of collection must be sound
- **Complete** — the full picture must be presented, not cherry-picked
- **Believable** — it must be understandable and credible to the court

**Challenges:** Digital evidence is highly **volatile** and can be easily modified, requiring skilled experts for collection.

---

### 4.2 Digital Signature

- Uses **Public Key Infrastructure (PKI)** and cryptography.
- Ensures **authenticity** — the signature can be verified as originating from the claimed sender.
- Ensures **integrity** — the content has not been altered after signing.
- Provides **non-repudiation** — the signer cannot later deny having signed.

---

### 4.3 E-Governance and E-Contracts

**E-Governance:** The application of ICT to improve government services (G2C, G2B, G2G, G2E).

- _Example:_ Online tax payment and NID smart card systems.

**E-Contracts:** Contracts created and signed online, often between parties who never meet physically.

**UNCITRAL Model Law:** A global mechanism passed in 1996 to harmonize rules on electronic commerce and signatures. It serves as the international foundation that many countries — including Bangladesh — have drawn upon to develop their own national cyber and e-commerce laws.

> 📌 **UNCITRAL** stands for the United Nations Commission on International Trade Law. Its Model Law on Electronic Commerce (1996) is a globally recognized framework that promotes legal equivalence between electronic and paper-based documents.

## Chapter 5

## Intellectual Property Law

### 5.1 Overview and Basic Concepts of IP Law

Intellectual Property Law (IPL) provides rules for securing legal rights to inventions, designs, and artistic works. The **World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO)** is the specialized UN agency promoting IP protection worldwide.

**Nature of IPR:** Intellectual Property Rights are exclusive rights granted to creators and inventors to use, reproduce, and commercialize their creations for a defined period. They are intangible rights — they protect products of the mind rather than physical property.

---

### 5.2 Computer-Related Intellectual Property Rights

Computer-related intellectual property rights apply IPL principles specifically to the digital domain:

|Right|Application to Computing|
|---|---|
|**Copyright**|Protects software source code, user interfaces, and digital content as literary or artistic works|
|**Patent**|Protects novel algorithms and technology inventions (where applicable by jurisdiction)|
|**Trademark**|Protects brand names, logos, and domain names of technology companies|
|**Semiconductor IC Layout Design**|Specifically protects the original design of integrated circuits to prevent unauthorized reproduction|

> 📌 **IPR** — Intellectual Property Rights are exclusive legal rights granted to creators over the use of their creations for a defined period. They are granted to encourage innovation by ensuring creators can benefit financially from their work.

---

### 5.3 Core Pillars of IP

1. **Copyright:** Protects original literary and artistic works (books, music, software source code).
    - _Example:_ Software programs like Adobe Photoshop or Microsoft Windows.
2. **Patent:** Exclusive rights granted for new and useful inventions for a limited period (usually 20 years).
    - _Example:_ Google's PageRank algorithm or a new pharmaceutical formula.
3. **Trademark:** A unique sign (logo, word) distinguishing one enterprise's goods/services from others.
    - _Example:_ The Apple logo or the name "Coca-Cola".
4. **Industrial Designs:** Protect the visual/aesthetic appearance of a product.
5. **Semiconductor IC Layout Design:** Specifically protects the original design of integrated circuits to prevent unauthorized reproduction.

---

### 5.4 IP Infringement in Cyberspace

**Software Piracy:** Illegal copying or distribution of genuine programs.

**Cybersquatting:** Registering domain names similar to famous brands to profit from their reputation or mislead users.

- _Example:_ Registering "IBMM.com" to confuse users looking for IBM.

## Chapter 6

## Copyright

### 6.1 Origin and Development of Copyright Law

Copyright law originated to protect authors and creators from unauthorized reproduction of their works. It developed alongside the invention of the printing press in the 15th century and has expanded through international treaties such as the **Berne Convention** to cover a wide range of creative works in the digital age.

---

### 6.2 Subject Matter of Copyright Protection

Copyright protects **original** works of authorship. The subject matter includes:

- Literary works (books, articles, software source code)
- Musical works and recordings
- Artistic and graphic works
- Films and audiovisual works
- Broadcasts and digital content

> 📌 The key requirement for copyright protection is **originality** — the work must originate from the author and reflect a minimum degree of creativity. Ideas themselves are not protected, only the expression of those ideas.

---

### 6.3 Rights Protected by Copyright

|Right|Description|
|---|---|
|**Reproduction Right**|The exclusive right to copy or reproduce the work|
|**Distribution Right**|The right to sell, rent, or otherwise distribute copies|
|**Public Performance Right**|The right to perform or display the work publicly|
|**Adaptation Right**|The right to create derivative works (translations, modifications)|
|**Communication Right**|The right to make the work available to the public via digital networks|

---

### 6.4 Neighboring Rights

Neighboring rights (also called related rights) protect the interests of performers, producers of phonograms, and broadcasting organizations — parties who contribute to making a work available to the public, even if they are not the original author.

---

### 6.5 Limitations of Copyright Protection

Copyright is not absolute. Limitations include:

- **Fair Use / Fair Dealing:** Allows limited use of copyrighted material for purposes such as education, criticism, commentary, news reporting, and research without permission.
- **Duration:** Copyright is not perpetual — it expires after a defined period (generally the life of the author plus 50–70 years), after which the work enters the public domain.
- **Compulsory Licensing:** In certain circumstances, a license to use a copyrighted work may be granted without the owner's consent, subject to payment of a fee.

---

### 6.6 Piracy, Infringement, and Remedies

**Copyright Infringement:** Occurs when a person exercises rights reserved exclusively to the copyright holder without authorization.

**Piracy:** The large-scale unauthorized copying and distribution of copyrighted works, particularly prevalent with digital content and software.

|Remedy|Description|
|---|---|
|**Civil Remedies**|Injunctions to stop infringing activity, damages (actual or statutory), and account of profits|
|**Criminal Remedies**|Fines and imprisonment for willful infringement on a commercial scale|
|**Border Measures**|Seizure and destruction of infringing goods at customs|

## Chapter 7

## Patent

### 7.1 Patents and Technological Development

Patents incentivize innovation by granting inventors an exclusive right to their invention for a limited period in exchange for full public disclosure of how the invention works. This disclosure drives technological development by adding to the public pool of technical knowledge.

---

### 7.2 Requirements for Patentability and Ownership

For an invention to be patentable, it must satisfy the following requirements:

|Requirement|Description|
|---|---|
|**Novelty**|The invention must be new — not previously disclosed to the public anywhere in the world|
|**Inventive Step (Non-obviousness)**|The invention must not be obvious to a person skilled in the relevant field|
|**Industrial Applicability (Utility)**|The invention must be capable of being used or applied in some kind of industry|

**Ownership of Patents:**

- The patent generally belongs to the **inventor**.
- In employment contexts, the employer may own the patent if the invention was made in the course of employment duties.
- Ownership can be transferred or licensed.

---

### 7.3 Scope of Exclusive Rights and Duration of Protection

A patent grants the holder the exclusive right to:

- **Make**, **use**, **sell**, **import**, and **export** the patented invention.

The standard duration of patent protection is **20 years** from the filing date, after which the invention enters the public domain and may be freely used by anyone.

---

### 7.4 Patent Infringement, Defenses, and Remedies

**Patent Infringement:** Occurs when a third party performs any of the exclusive acts (making, using, selling) without the patent holder's authorization, within the jurisdiction and during the protection period.

**Defenses:**

- **Experimental Use:** Using the patented invention solely for research or experimental purposes.
- **Prior Use:** A party who was already using the invention before the patent was filed may have a defense.
- **Invalidity:** The defendant may challenge the validity of the patent itself (e.g., arguing it lacks novelty).

|Remedy|Description|
|---|---|
|**Injunction**|Court order to stop the infringing activity|
|**Damages**|Compensation for losses suffered due to infringement|
|**Account of Profits**|The infringer must hand over the profits made from the infringement|
|**Seizure**|Infringing goods may be seized and destroyed|

## Chapter 8

## Trademarks

### 8.1 Reasons for the Protection of Trademarks

A trademark is a unique sign (logo, word, symbol, or combination) that distinguishes one enterprise's goods or services from those of others. Trademarks are protected for two key reasons:

- **Consumer Protection:** They enable consumers to identify and rely on the source and quality of goods and services.
- **Business Protection:** They protect the goodwill and commercial reputation that a business builds under its mark.

---

### 8.2 Acquisition of Trademark Right

Trademark rights can be acquired in two ways:

|Method|Description|
|---|---|
|**Use**|Rights arise automatically through actual use of the mark in commerce (common law trademark)|
|**Registration**|Rights are formally established by registering the mark with the national trademark office|

> 💡 **Cybersquatting** is a trademark-related infringement in cyberspace. It involves registering domain names that are identical or confusingly similar to well-known trademarks, with the intent to profit from the trademark owner's reputation. _Example:_ Registering "IBMM.com" to confuse users looking for IBM.

---

### 8.3 Registration Procedure

The trademark registration process generally involves the following steps:

1. **Filing an application** with the relevant trademark office, including a representation of the mark and the classes of goods/services it will cover.
2. **Examination** — the office examines the mark for compliance with formal requirements and checks for conflicts with existing marks.
3. **Publication** — the mark is published in an official gazette to allow third parties to oppose the registration.
4. **Opposition period** — any party with a legitimate interest may oppose the registration within a defined period.
5. **Registration** — if no opposition is successful, the mark is registered and a certificate is issued.

---

### 8.4 Duration of Protection and Renewal

- Trademark protection is typically granted for an initial period of **10 years** from the date of registration.
- Unlike patents and copyrights, trademarks can be **renewed indefinitely** — as long as the renewal fees are paid and the mark continues to be used in commerce.

---

### 8.5 Termination of Trademark Rights

Trademark rights can be terminated or lost in the following circumstances:

|Ground|Description|
|---|---|
|**Non-renewal**|Failure to pay renewal fees causes the registration to lapse|
|**Non-use**|Extended non-use of the trademark (typically 3–5 years) may result in cancellation|
|**Genericization**|If a trademark becomes the common name for a type of product, it may lose protection (e.g., "Escalator" and "Aspirin" were once trademarks)|
|**Abandonment**|The owner expressly abandons the mark|
|**Cancellation**|A court or trademark office cancels the mark due to invalidity or successful third-party challenge|
