## Chapter 1
## Foundations of Business, Commerce, and Industry

### 1.1 Business

**Definition**: An organization or entity engaged in commercial, industrial, or professional activities to produce, sell, or exchange goods or services for profit.

**Key Characteristics**:
- **Profit Motive**: The primary goal is to generate revenue exceeding costs.
- **Risk and Uncertainty**: Operates in dynamic environments with inherent risks like competition and economic shifts.
- **Legal Existence**: Typically operates within a legal framework and can sue or be sued.

**Meaning & Objectives of Business**:
- **Meaning**: Business refers to all human activities directed towards producing and distributing wealth through the purchase and sale of goods.
- **Objectives**:
    - Economic Objectives: Earning profits, creating customers, and innovation.
    - Social Objectives: Providing quality goods, fair prices, and employment.
    - Human Objectives: Employee welfare and satisfaction.
    - National Objectives: Paying taxes and contributing to national development.

**Types of Business Entities**:
- **Sole Proprietorship**: Owned and operated by one individual who has unlimited personal liability.
- **Partnership**: Two or more individuals share ownership, profits, and liabilities.
- **Corporation (Joint Stock Company)**: A legal entity separate from its owners (shareholders) with limited liability and perpetual existence.
- **State Enterprise**: An enterprise owned and operated by the government.
- **Cooperative Society**: A voluntary association of persons formed for mutual benefit and service.

### 1.2 Commerce

**Definition**: The activities involved in the exchange of goods and services between producers and consumers.

**Function**: Facilitates trade by removing barriers related to time, place, and possession.

**Types**: Includes e-commerce (online transactions), international trade, retail, and wholesale.

### 1.3 Industry

**Meaning & Characteristics of Industry:**
- **Meaning**: A sector of the economy focused on producing goods or providing services within a specific category.
- **Characteristics**:
    - Involves the conversion of raw materials into finished goods.
    - Creates form utility.
    - Requires capital investment and labor.

**Types of Industry**:
- **Primary/Extractive**: Involved in extracting natural resources (e.g., mining, agriculture, oil extraction).
- **Secondary/Manufacturing**: Processes raw materials into finished goods (e.g., textile production, steel manufacturing).
- **Tertiary/Service**: Provides intangible services like healthcare, finance, or education.

## Chapter 2
## Fundamentals of Management

### 2.1 Nature of Management

**Meaning of Management**: The process of planning, organizing, leading, and controlling resources (human, financial, physical, and information) to achieve organizational goals efficiently and effectively.

**Efficiency vs. Effectiveness**: Efficiency (Means) focuses on low resource waste, while Effectiveness (Ends) focuses on high goal attainment.

**Principles of Management (Henri Fayol’s 14 Principles)** : Includes Division of Work, Authority and Responsibility, Discipline, Unity of Command, and Esprit de Corps.

### 2.2 Managerial Skills and Roles

**Core Management skills (Three Essential Skills by Robert Katz)** :
1.  **Technical**: Proficiency in specific procedures and techniques (crucial for supervisory levels).
2.  **Human**: The ability to work with and motivate people.
3.  **Conceptual**: The ability to see the organization as a whole and coordinate integrated interests (crucial for top management).

**Roles of Management (Mintzberg’s Roles)** : Managers act as **Interpersonal** (figurehead, leader), **Informational** (monitor, spokesperson), and **Decisional** (entrepreneur, negotiator) actors.

### 2.3 Core Functions of Management

- **Planning**: Selecting missions, objectives, and actions to achieve them; "deciding in advance what is to be done".
- **Organizing**: Creating an organizational structure by identifying tasks, assigning roles, and delegating authority.
- **Leading/Directing**: Motivating and influencing employees to work toward group goals.
- **Controlling**: Monitoring performance, comparing it with goals, and taking corrective action.

### 2.4 Levels of Management

- **Top Management**: Sets goals, policies, and strategies (e.g., CEO, Board of Directors).
- **Middle Management**: Executes plans and coordinates departmental activities.
- **Lower/Supervisory Management**: Oversees daily operations and direct workers.

### 2.5 Scientific Management

- Developed by Frederick W. Taylor.
- Focuses on using scientific methods to determine the best way to do a job.
- Key elements: Time and motion studies, differential piece-rate system, and standardization of tools.

## Chapter 4
## Work-Environment and Plant Utility

### 4.1 Work Environment

**Meaning**: The sum total of physical, social, and psychological conditions affecting workers in a workplace.

**Importance**:
- Improves employee morale.
- Increases productivity.
- Reduces accidents and absenteeism.

**Factors Affecting Work Environment**:
- Lighting
- Ventilation
- Air-conditioning
- Sanitation
- Noise Control
- Safety measures
- Cleanliness

### 4.2 Plant Utility

**Plant Utility**: Facilities and services that make the plant habitable and operational.

**Lighting**: Proper illumination to prevent eye strain and accidents.
**Ventilation**: Exchange of air to remove stale air and odors.
**Air-conditioning**: Control of temperature and humidity for comfort and precision work.
**Sanitation**: Clean drinking water, restrooms, and waste disposal systems.
**Noise Control**: Reducing sound levels using absorbers, silencers, or barriers.

## Chapter 5
## Sole Proprietorships

**Features**:
- Single ownership
- Unlimited liability
- No legal distinction between the owner and the business
- Sole risk-bearing and profit-taking

**Advantages of Sole Proprietorship**:
1.  Easy to form and dissolve.
2.  Direct incentive (all profits go to owner).
3.  Personal touch with customers.
4.  Quick decision-making.
5.  Secrecy of business affairs.

**Disadvantages of Sole Proprietorship**:
1.  Unlimited personal liability for debts.
2.  Limited capital and resources.
3.  Limited managerial expertise.
4.  Lack of continuity (business ends with owner's death or illness).

**Sustainability of Sole proprietorships**:
- Suitable for small-scale, local, and personalized services (e.g., retail shops, repair shops).
- Sustains through customer loyalty, niche markets, and adaptability.

## Chapter 6
## Man Power Planning & Motivation

### 6.1 Manpower Planning

**Need**: To ensure the right number of people with the right skills at the right time.

**Objectives**:
- To forecast future personnel needs.
- To avoid shortages or surpluses of labor.
- To optimize utilization of human resources.

**Manpower Planning Process**:
1.  Analyzing current manpower inventory.
2.  Forecasting future demand and supply.
3.  Reconciling demand and supply.
4.  Implementing the plan through recruitment, selection, and training.

**Recruitment**: Process of finding and attracting potential candidates. (Internal: promotion, transfer; External: campus, ads).
**Selection**: Process of choosing the most suitable candidate (steps: tests, interview, reference check).
**Training**: Systematic development of knowledge, skills, and attitudes (On-the-job, Off-the-job).

**Issue in Managing People**:
- Resistance to change.
- Managing diversity.
- Work-life balance.
- Employee turnover.

### 6.2 Motivation Theories

**Maslow’s Need Hierarchy**: Needs are arranged from Physiological, Safety, Affiliation (Social), and Esteem to Self-actualization.

**Social Needs and Productivity**: When affiliation and social needs are met, employees experience a sense of belonging, which increases collaboration and productivity.

**Hygiene and Motivators (Herzberg’s Two-Factor Theory)** :
- **Hygiene Factors (Maintenance Factors)** : Dissatisfiers if absent (e.g., salary, work conditions, company policies).
- **Motivators (Satisfiers)** : Satisfiers that lead to high motivation and job satisfaction (e.g., achievement, recognition, responsibility, growth).

**McGregor’s Theory X & Y**: Theory X assumes people dislike work and need coercion; Theory Y assumes work is natural and people seek responsibility.

**Vroom’s Expectancy Theory**: Motivation ($Force$) = Valence $\times$ Expectancy (the value of the outcome times the probability of achieving it).

## Chapter 7
## Conflict & Union Management Perspective

### 7.1 Conflict

**Meaning**: A process that begins when one party perceives that another party has negatively affected, or is about to negatively affect, something the first party cares about.

**Process of Conflict**:
1.  Latent Conflict (underlying conditions).
2.  Perceived Conflict (awareness).
3.  Felt Conflict (emotional involvement).
4.  Manifest Conflict (action, open confrontation).
5.  Conflict Aftermath (resolution or suppression).

**Types of Conflict**:
- **Intrapersonal**: Within an individual.
- **Interpersonal**: Between two or more individuals.
- **Intragroup**: Within a team or department.
- **Intergroup**: Between different groups or departments.
- **Industrial**: Between management and workers/unions.

**Industrial Conflict Resolution Methods**:
- **Collective Bargaining**: Negotiation between employer and union.
- **Grievance Procedure**: A formal step-by-step process for handling complaints.
- **Mediation**: A neutral third party helps disputants reach a voluntary agreement.
- **Arbitration**: A neutral third party hears evidence and imposes a binding decision.
- **Conciliation**: A third party meets with the parties separately to find common ground.

**Negotiation Skills**:
- Preparation and planning.
- Active listening.
- Clear communication.
- Problem-solving attitude.
- Emotional control.

### 7.2 Trade Union Management Perspective

**Growth of Trade Unions**: Grew due to industrialization, poor working conditions, long hours, low wages, and the need for collective voice.

**Functions of Trade Unions**:
- **Militant Functions**: Securing better wages, working conditions, and fringe benefits through collective bargaining and industrial action.
- **Fraternal Functions**: Providing welfare, education, recreation, and mutual insurance to members.

**Structure**:
- **Craft Union**: Represents workers of a specific skill.
- **Industrial Union**: Represents all workers in a particular industry.
- **General Union**: Represents workers from any industry or skill.

**Leadership and Management in the Trade Union**:
- Leadership is often elected from among the members.
- Management of the union involves maintaining records, collecting dues, and holding meetings.

**Collective Bargaining**: The process of negotiation between employer(s) and a group of employees (represented by a union) to determine wages, hours, rules, and working conditions.

## Chapter 8
## Accounting: Foundations and Transactions

### 8.1 Accountings

**History**: Evolved from simple record-keeping (Luca Pacioli documented double-entry system in 1494) to a sophisticated information system.

**Scope and Nature of Accounting**:
- **Scope**: Covers recording, classifying, summarizing, analyzing, and interpreting financial transactions.
- **Nature**: It is an art (recording) and a science (principles), a service activity, and an information system.

**Purpose of Accounting**:
- To keep a systematic record of transactions.
- To ascertain profit or loss (Income Statement).
- To ascertain financial position (Balance Sheet).
- To provide information to users for decision-making.

**Information and Uses**:
- **Internal Users**: Managers, owners for planning and control.
- **External Users**: Investors, creditors, tax authorities, customers, employees.

### 8.2 Transaction

**Meaning**: An economic event that affects the financial position of a business and can be measured reliably in monetary terms.

**Features**:
- It has a money value.
- It involves a transfer of goods, services, or money.
- It changes the accounting equation.

**Accounting Equation**:
**Assets = Liabilities + Owner’s Equity**.
- *Example*: If a startup "Abid Computers" invests Tk. 300,000 cash (Asset), Equity increases by Tk. 300,000.

**Meaning and Classification of Account**:
- **Meaning**: A record of increases and decreases in a specific asset, liability, equity, revenue, or expense item.
- **Classification (Traditional Approach)** :
    1.  **Personal Accounts**: Persons (e.g., Ram, SBI).
    2.  **Impersonal Accounts**:
        - Real Accounts (Assets: Cash, Machinery).
        - Nominal Accounts (Expenses, Losses, Incomes, Gains).

**Double entry System**: Every transaction has two aspects, a debit and a credit for equal amounts.

**Rules for Determining Debit and Credit (Modern Approach)** :

| Type of Account | Increase | Decrease |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Asset** | Debit | Credit |
| **Expense** | Debit | Credit |
| **Drawings** | Debit | Credit |
| **Liability** | Credit | Debit |
| **Revenue** | Credit | Debit |
| **Capital** | Credit | Debit |

**Accounting cycle**: The series of steps from transaction analysis to preparing financial statements and closing the books.

## Chapter 9
## Journal, Ledger and Trial Balance

### 9.1 Journal

**Meaning**: The "book of original entry" where transactions are recorded in chronological order.

**Features**:
- Shows the complete transaction in one entry.
- Shows debit and credit aspects clearly.
- Provides a date-wise record.

**Necessity**: Prevents omission and provides a legal record.

**Rules**: Debit the receiver (for personal a/c), Credit the giver. Debit what comes in (Real a/c), Credit what goes out. Debit all expenses/losses, Credit all incomes/gains.

**Double and Triple Column Cash Book**:
- **Double Column**: Shows discount and cash.
- **Triple Column**: Shows discount, cash, and bank.

**Practical Problems**: (Note: The curriculum includes practical problems. Study of journalizing various transactions like purchase, sale, payment, receipt, and return of goods is required.)

### 9.2 Ledger

**Meaning**: The entire group of accounts maintained by a company; contains the "T-accounts" showing increases and decreases.

**Posting**: The procedure of transferring journal entries to the ledger accounts.

### 9.3 Trial Balance

**Meaning**: A schedule listing all ledger account balances (debits and credits) prepared to test the arithmetic accuracy of the books.

**Features**: Debit balances (Assets, Expenses) and Credit balances (Liabilities, Revenue, Capital).

**Necessity**: Proves that total debits equal total credits.

## Chapter 10
## Work Sheet

**Meaning**: A multiple-column sheet used as a preliminary tool for preparing adjusting entries and financial statements.

**Purpose**:
- Simplifies the preparation of financial statements.
- Facilitates the making of adjusting entries.
- Reduces the chance of omission.

**Adjustment Entries**: Entries made at the end of an accounting period to record revenues earned but not recorded and expenses incurred but not recorded (e.g., depreciation, prepaid expenses, accrued income).

**10 Columns Work Sheet**: Typically contains the following pairs of columns:
1.  Trial Balance (Debit & Credit)
2.  Adjustments (Debit & Credit)
3.  Adjusted Trial Balance (Debit & Credit)
4.  Income Statement (Debit & Credit)
5.  Balance Sheet (Debit & Credit)

## Chapter 11
## Cost Terms Concepts and Classification

### 11.1 Meaning of Cost

**Meaning of Cost**: The amount of expenditure (actual or notional) incurred on or attributable to a specific thing or activity.

### 11.2 Classification of Costs

**Manufacturing and Non Manufacturing Costs**:
- **Manufacturing Costs (Product Costs)** : Costs incurred in the factory.
    - **Direct Materials**: Raw materials integral to the product (e.g., wood for furniture).
    - **Direct Labor**: Workers directly involved in production.
    - **Manufacturing Overhead**: Indirect costs (e.g., factory utilities, indirect materials like lubricants).
- **Non-Manufacturing Costs (Period Costs)** : Costs not related to production.
    - Selling Costs (advertising, sales salaries).
    - Administrative Costs (office salaries, legal fees).

**Period and Product Costs**:
- **Product Costs**: Attached to inventory; expensed as Cost of Goods Sold when sold (Direct Materials, Direct Labor, Overhead).
- **Period Costs**: Expensed immediately in the period incurred (Selling & Admin).

**Variable and Fixed Costs (Behavioral Classification)** :
- **Variable Cost**: Varies in total in direct proportion to activity levels but remains constant per unit.
- **Fixed Cost**: Remains constant in total regardless of activity changes.

**Direct and Indirect Costs**:
- **Direct Cost**: Can be traced directly to a cost object (e.g., salary of a department supervisor to that department).
- **Indirect Cost**: Cannot be traced directly; must be allocated (e.g., rent for a building shared by multiple departments).

**Differential, Opportunity and Sunk Costs**:
- **Differential Cost**: The difference in total cost between two alternatives.
- **Opportunity Cost**: The potential benefit given up when one alternative is chosen over another.
- **Sunk Cost**: A cost that has already been incurred and cannot be changed by any future decision.

### 11.3 Schedules and Statement

**Schedule of Cost of Goods Manufactured**:
Calculates the total cost of goods completed during the period.
Formula: Direct Materials Used + Direct Labor + Manufacturing Overhead + Beginning WIP – Ending WIP.

**Schedule of Cost of Goods Sold**:
Calculates the cost of goods sold during the period.
Formula: Beginning Finished Goods + Cost of Goods Manufactured – Ending Finished Goods.

**Income Statement**:
Profit = Sales – Cost of Goods Sold – Operating Expenses.

## Chapter 12
## Cost-Volume-Profit Relationship

### 12.1 Contribution Margin and Ratio

**Contribution Margin (CM)** : What remains from sales revenue after subtracting variable costs.
$$CM = Sales - Variable Costs$$

**Contribution Margin Ratio (CM Ratio)** :
$$CM \ Ratio = \frac{Contribution \ Margin}{Sales}$$

### 12.2 Break-even Analysis

**Break-even Point (BEP)** : The level of sales where total revenue equals total expenses, resulting in zero profit.
$$BEP \ (units) = \frac{Fixed \ Costs}{CM \ per \ unit}$$
- *CSE Example*: If an IT company has Tk. 1,60,000 fixed costs and a Tk. 30 CM per unit, the BEP is 5,333.33 units.

**Break-even in Sales Dollars**:
$$BEP \ (dollars) = \frac{Fixed \ Costs}{CM \ Ratio}$$

### 12.3 CVP relationship in Graphical Form

The graph plots:
1.  **Total Revenue Line** (starting from zero, slope = selling price).
2.  **Total Cost Line** (starting at Fixed Cost, slope = variable cost per unit).
3.  **Break-even Point**: The intersection of TR and TC.
4.  **Loss Area**: Where TR < TC.
5.  **Profit Area**: Where TR > TC.
6.  **Margin of Safety**: The actual or budgeted sales in excess of the break-even sales.

### 12.4 Target Net Profit Analysis

*   **Target Net Profit Analysis**: Determining the sales volume needed to achieve a specific level of profit.

**Unit Sales for Target Profit**:
$$Units \ for \ Target \ Profit = \frac{Fixed \ Costs + Target \ Profit}{CM \ per \ unit}$$

**Dollar Sales for Target Profit**:
$$Sales \ \$ \ for \ Target \ Profit = \frac{Fixed \ Costs + Target \ Profit}{CM \ Ratio}$$
